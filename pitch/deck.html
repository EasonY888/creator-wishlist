The gap we close

The merchant's final total is not knowable in advance.

The shop adds tax at checkout. So “the fan approves a total” cannot be implemented literally — and the obvious workaround is wrong twice.

The obvious implementation

  • Authorise a ceiling, capture the whole thing
  • Refund the difference afterwards
  • Two statement lines for one gift
  • A refund fee on every order
  • A window where the fan's money is simply gone

What we do instead

  • Fan approves a ceiling, and the UI says so
  • We hold the money — we don't take it
  • The shop's real total comes back from the settlement
  • We capture once, for the number we showed
  • The difference is released, not refunded
A ceiling is never presented as a total. When the shop charges less, the saving goes back to the fan — we do not keep the difference.

The promise, proven

The creator's address never reaches the fan.

CreatorAddress.ciphertext
v1:9fKq2R...A7mZ0t...x81LpQ==

// AES-256-GCM, encrypted before the row is written.
// A missing key throws. There is no plaintext fallback.

The digest beside it is deliberately not encrypted — it is taken over the plaintext. If it moved with the encryption key, rotating that key would silently cancel every outstanding approval. The order carries the digest, never the address.

Fan payload
no address field exists to leak — not redacted, absent
Operator payload
...refused: postal_address.street_address, address_locality, postal_code

The guard names the offending fields and refuses to build the payload — and there is a test that fails if it ever becomes vacuous. When the creator moves house, the outstanding order refuses rather than shipping to the wrong door.

Provenance & scope

What is real, and what is deliberately scoped.

Real, end to end

  • A real merchant settlement: the shop was charged 13.00 through Agnic — af_ord_mu8yhh4hhi7zo6az
  • A real Stripe PaymentIntent in test mode, authorised then captured — pi_3UHWgs…1Z59KStW
  • Real address encryption at rest, AES-256-GCM, with no plaintext path
  • Real operator authentication, real refund route, real audit trail

Scoped, and stated as such

  • The exhibit order's fan rail ran on the in-memory double — the pay_… refs show it. That is what lets us show the bug without moving money
  • Stripe is in test mode: the amounts are real, the funds are not
  • One sandbox merchant's gateway accepts the test card; its storefront has no public product URLs, so its catalogue is synced by script
